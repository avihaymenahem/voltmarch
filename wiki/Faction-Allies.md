# Allied Forces

> Precision, mobility and beam tech. Fewer, better units.

Cobalt and chrome. Chamfered plate, cool cyan panel glow, no rivets anywhere — Allied hardware is
machined rather than welded, and it shows in the silhouette before it shows in the stats. The
faction's thesis is that a gun that reaches further, hits more precisely and costs less does not
need to be armoured like a bunker. That is true right up until somebody catches an Allied column in
the open, at which point it stops being true very quickly.

The Allies are the engineering answer. Where the Soviets buy hit points, the Allies buy beams: the
Prism Emitter ignores almost every armour class in the game (0.95 against Light and Medium, 0.90
against Heavy), which is why the two units and one tower that carry it are expensive, slow, and have
to stand still to shoot. Everything else in the roster exists to keep them alive long enough to
fire.

They also own the only dedicated anti-air turret in the game, alongside a cheap machine-gun nest and
a heavy beam tower, and two superweapons that solve two different problems: the Displacement Ring puts
your army somewhere it should not be, and the Weather Control Device denies a piece of the map for
nine seconds.

## How it plays

Cheap medium armour, fast. The Warden is 700 credits against the Soviet Anvil's 900 and builds in
11 seconds against 13, so for the price of two Anvils you field two and a half Wardens. They are
also Medium armour, not Heavy, and armour-piercing rounds hit Medium at exactly the same 1.00 they
hit Heavy — so an Anvil does not lose damage against you the way you lose damage against it. The
trade is speed (6.6 m/s against 5.4) and count.

Your infantry can shoot up. The G.I.'s carbine and the Javelin's shoulder rocket both elevate, which
means an Allied ground force is never completely helpless against aircraft even before you build a
turret.

Your specialist units are glass. The Refractor Tank is 1200 credits for 260 hit points behind Light
armour, must stop to fire, and cannot shoot at aircraft at all. Nor can the Field Marshal, who
carries the same emitter. Screen them or lose them.

The Wall stops vehicles and nothing else — infantry walk straight through a wall line, so a Gate is
for your own convenience rather than theirs. **The Pillbox is the only Allied gun that survives a
blackout**, because it is the only one that draws no power — the Refractor Tower and the AA Battery
both go dark with the grid. That is why a 400-credit box stays useful all match.

**The AA Battery is the only dedicated anti-air emplacement any faction in the game has.** That
is a genuine Allied advantage and worth building the moment aircraft appear.

## Superweapons

Both need the Proving Ground, both cost -150 power, and you can field both at once.

| Structure | Cost | Time | HP | Charge | Radius | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Displacement Ring | 2000 | 28 s | 950 | 5:00 | 11 m | Two clicks — a source and a destination. Up to nine friendly ground units are lifted out of one and set down in the other |
| Weather Control Device | 2500 | 32 s | 1000 | 6:40 | 16 m | Nine seconds of lightning, roughly a bolt every 0.42 s at 190 damage with 4.5 m splash. More total damage than a nuke; none of it lands where you thought |

The Displacement Ring is the only superweapon in the game that moves your own army rather than damaging
someone else's. Nine tanks appearing behind a defensive line is a different kind of threat from
anything the other three armies can pose.

## Commander

**Field Marshal** — 1500 credits, 20 seconds, needs a Barracks and a Radar Dome. 460 HP, 3.8 m/s,
34 m of sight, and he carries a full Prism Emitter (92 damage at 30 m), which makes him a serious
gun in his own right. He cannot shoot at aircraft.

**Chrono Rally** — 50-second cooldown, 34 m radius. Teleports up to six nearby friendly units to a
ring around the commander. The longest reach of any commander ability in the game by a wide margin,
and the only one that repositions rather than damages or protects. Use it to pull a scattered push
back together, or to yank a wounded column out of a fight in one motion.

## Strengths

- The cheapest main battle tank of the two shared-pool armies, at 700 credits and 11 seconds.
- The only dedicated anti-air emplacement in the game.
- Three armed emplacements covering three different jobs: a cheap anti-infantry nest, a flak
  battery and a long beam tower.
- Prism weapons ignore almost all armour scaling: 0.95 against Light and Medium, 0.90 against
  Heavy, 1.10 against infantry.
- Rifles and rocket launchers both elevate, so Allied infantry are never blind to aircraft.
- Two superweapons that answer different problems.

## Weaknesses

- Medium armour on the line tank. Armour-piercing hits Medium at the same 1.00 it hits Heavy, so
  you take full damage from every tank gun in the game while the Soviets do not.
- Every beam unit — Refractor Tank, Refractor Tower, Field Marshal — must stop to fire, and neither the
  Refractor Tank nor the Field Marshal can shoot at aircraft.
- The Refractor Tank is 1200 credits for 260 hit points. It dies to a raid it never sees coming.
- Only one cheap early defence (the Pillbox) where the Soviets have two.
- Only two of your units crush infantry (Warden, Harvester); the Refractor Tank does not.
- Much of the interesting roster — IFV, Refractor Tank, Petrel Bomber, the Proving Ground and everything behind
  it — is behind mission unlocks on a fresh profile. The navy is not: docks, hulls and the Frogman
  are all day-one.
